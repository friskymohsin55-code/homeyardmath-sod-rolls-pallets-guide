How Many Sod Rolls and Pallets Does Your Lawn Need?

By Syed Mohsin Ali — HomeYardMath

Ordering sod is simple once you know the two pallet sizes. Northern cool-season farms pack 500 sq ft per pallet (50 rolls of 10 sq ft); southern warm-season farms pack 450 sq ft. Assume the wrong one and you arrive 10% short on delivery day — three "500 sq ft" pallets from a 450 sq ft farm leaves you 150 sq ft short.

A real worked example

Take a 50 ft × 20 ft lawn — 1,000 sq ft. Add the standard 10% waste allowance for cuts around beds and paths and you need 1,100 sq ft of sod. That is 110 rolls at $5.50 each, or $605.00. By pallet: three 500 sq ft pallets at $275 each is $825, or three 450 sq ft pallets at $245 each is $735. The smart order is 2 pallets of 500 sq ft plus 10 rolls: $550 + $55 = $605, with almost no leftover. Use a free sod roll and pallet calculator to run your own lawn size before you pay.

Delivery and timing

Sod runs about 5 lbs per sq ft, so 1,100 sq ft weighs roughly 5,500 lbs — a single wet pallet weighs 2,000 to 3,000 lbs, so never load a full pallet into a half-ton pickup. Budget the $110 forklift delivery fee and have the driver place pallets across the yard. Lay every roll within 24 hours of delivery (12 hours in 85°F-plus heat), or the center rolls cook on the pallet.

Plan the rest of the yard the same way: work out how much pea gravel you need for paths and patios, and compare mulch bags vs bulk before you buy — bulk wins once the job passes about a cubic yard.
